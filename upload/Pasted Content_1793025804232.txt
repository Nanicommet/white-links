## 2. PRODUCT VISION

White Links is a recurring 14-day anonymous kindness game designed to bring the members of White Line Club ISSIG closer together.

Every participant is secretly assigned another participant to take care of.

During the game, participants can:
- Send anonymous messages.
- Make their secret friend smile.
- Leave small gifts or kind notes.
- Perform thoughtful gestures.
- Receive anonymous messages from the person secretly assigned to them.
- Try to guess the identity of their secret admirer during the final reveal.

The game runs in recurring rounds. Registration opens for 48 hours, the active game lasts 14 days, and the system manages the transition to the next round.

### Brand identity

Name: WHITE LINKS

Primary tagline: "De petites attentions. Des liens invisibles."

Secondary English tagline: "Small gestures. Secret connections."

Brand personality:
- Mysterious but welcoming.
- Playful but elegant.
- Friendly, caring, and trustworthy.
- Young and modern, reflecting a university club.
- Premium, polished, and thoughtfully designed.

Make the application feel like entering a private world of secret missions, anonymous messages, envelopes, surprises, and hidden connections.

Use subtle animations, elegant cards, thoughtful empty states, smooth transitions, and a refined visual hierarchy. Avoid childish cartoon styling, generic AI-generated dashboards, excessive gradients, and unnecessary gamification.

The design must remain consistent with the real White Line Club brand. Sample the colors and typography from the existing website whenever possible rather than assuming exact color values.

## 5. ROUND LIFECYCLE

Implement a server-enforced state machine.

Suggested statuses:
- `draft`
- `registration_open`
- `registration_closed`
- `matching_pending`
- `active`
- `guessing_open`
- `revealed`
- `archived`

Define valid transitions and enforce them in backend logic.

Registration should be open for exactly 48 hours by default.

Once registration closes:
1. Freeze the eligible participant list.
2. Validate registrations and matching constraints.
3. Generate assignments using a secure server-side algorithm.
4. Verify all assignment invariants.
5. Save the assignment set atomically.
6. Notify participants only after finalization succeeds.
7. Transition the round to active.

Use database transactions or an equivalent atomic operation when finalizing assignments.

Do not allow two concurrent requests to generate conflicting assignments. Assignment finalization must be idempotent: retries must not create another set of assignments.

The active game lasts 14 days by default.

Allow a configurable guessing window on the final day. Reveal should happen only after the guessing deadline has passed.

Support advance registration for the next round during the final 48 hours of the current round, if administrators enable it. However, do not finalize the next round's assignments until its participant list, any eligible reward preferences, and matching constraints have been validated.

Use timezone `Africa/Tunis` for scheduling and display dates in the appropriate local timezone.

Use backend timestamps as the source of truth.

## 6. MATCHING ENGINE — CRITICAL

The matching engine is the most important part of the product.

Every participant must have:
- Exactly one outgoing assignment: the person they take care of.
- Exactly one incoming assignment: the person who takes care of them.

These invariants must be enforced server-side.

### Matching mode A: Cross-category matching

Provide a configurable cross-gender matching rule consistent with the club's chosen policy.

If the rule is enabled:
- Each participant in group A must be assigned to a participant in group B.
- Each participant in group B must be assigned to a participant in group A.
- Both eligible groups must have equal participant counts.
- Each participant appears exactly once as a giver and exactly once as a recipient.
- Self-assignment is impossible.

If participant counts are unequal, do not finalize assignments. Display a clear administrator error explaining the imbalance and the options for resolving it.

### Matching mode B: Open matching

Provide an alternative mode in which anyone may be assigned to another eligible participant regardless of matching category.

Generate a randomized derangement: no participant is assigned to themselves, each person receives exactly one giver, and every participant gives to exactly one other participant.

The mode must respect any supported exclusion preferences.

### General assignment requirements

- Validate all constraints on the server.
- Do not generate assignments when there are too few eligible participants.
- Prevent a participant from appearing multiple times as a recipient.
- Avoid obvious reciprocal pairs when feasible and when doing so does not violate the other constraints.
- If preferences or exclusions make a valid assignment impossible, return a clear error rather than silently violating a rule.
- Revalidate assignments before committing them.
- Verify invariants after saving.
- Do not notify participants of failed or uncommitted assignments.

Support a secure assignment repair process for withdrawals or exceptional cases after the game starts. Reassignment must preserve all one-to-one invariants and must not expose other people's hidden assignments. Require administrator authorization and an audit record.

### Reward preference matching

Participants who correctly guess their admirer earn priority to request a preferred recipient in the next round.

Let winners rank up to three eligible participants.

Use these ranked preferences to guide the next matching algorithm while preserving the matching constraints.

If multiple winners select the same person:
- Apply a transparent priority rule.
- Use a secure randomized tie-break when required.
- Try the winners' next-ranked preferences.
- Complete the remaining assignments randomly.
- Guarantee that every participant still has exactly one giver and one recipient.

Do not guarantee a requested person if accepting that preference would make valid matching impossible. Explain the reward rules before the guessing period opens.

**Reply to my secret admirer:** route the response to the participant identified by their incoming assignment.

- Send it from the official White Links business number.

- Do not include the original sender's name.
- Do not include the original sender's WhatsApp number.
- Do not leak internal participant IDs or assignment records.
- Label the message as coming from the participant's anonymous secret friend.
- Store the actual sender and recipient association securely in the backend for authorization and moderation.

Use approved WhatsApp templates where the provider requires them. Respect applicable message-session rules, opt-in requirements, rate limits, and provider policies.

## 8. AI MESSAGE MODERATION

Implement an AI moderation service using a secure server-side function and a configurable AI provider.

The AI moderates messages. It must never generate, modify, or decide assignments.

Before forwarding a message:
1. Validate the registered sender and round status.
2. Apply deterministic filters for obvious prohibited content.
3. Detect phone numbers, email addresses, URLs, social media handles, and other potential contact details.
4. Ask the AI to classify the message for attempts to reveal identities, explicit identity hints, harassment, threats, sexual harassment, discriminatory abuse, prohibited contact exchange, and attempts to bypass game rules.
5. Decide whether to allow, reject, or send the message to a human moderation queue.
6. Forward only an accepted message.
7. Record the moderation result and delivery status.

Require structured moderation output, such as:
- `decision`: `allow`, `reject`, or `review`.
- `reason_code`.
- `safe_explanation`.
- `confidence`.

Do not display private chain-of-thought or hidden reasoning.

Do not send participant phone numbers, secret assignment mappings, or unnecessary personal profile information to the AI provider. Send only the minimum information required to classify the message.

The moderation service must:
- Detect direct and obvious attempts to identify the sender.
- Flag requests to exchange personal social-media accounts or phone numbers.
- Flag harassment, coercion, and threatening messages.
- Avoid blocking ordinary compliments, jokes, kind messages, or natural Tunisian Arabic, French, and Arabic expressions unnecessarily.
- Allow a participant to rewrite a rejected message.
- Queue uncertain cases for review.
- Apply rate limits and prevent automated message floods.
- Provide participants with a simple explanation when a message is rejected.

If the AI provider is not configured or the moderation request fails, do not silently bypass moderation. Place messages into a safe pending state or use another explicitly configured moderation process.

Be transparent that AI moderation cannot guarantee detection of every indirect identity clue.

Provide a report and block mechanism so a participant can raise a concern with the club's authorized moderators.

Do not use private messages for unrelated model training or analytics.
